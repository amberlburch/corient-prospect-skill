# Trigger library

A trigger is the answer to "why today". The playbook's numbers:

- Trigger-event openers lift replies 40 to 65%. Role-pain openers 20 to 35%. Generic
  compliments 5 to 10%.
- Signals go stale after roughly 7 days.
- Two stacked signals produce 4.7x the positive replies of one.

So: check freshness before drafting, and try to stack. A trigger outside its window is worse
than no trigger, because it dates the research and tells them this was queued.

---

## The library

| Trigger | Where to detect it | Window | Opening line shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Funding round** | Crunchbase, LinkedIn company posts, AFR, StartupDaily, Business News WA | Days 7 to 45. Day 0 to 7 is congratulations noise from everyone. | The spend decision the round creates, not the round. "Post-raise the growth hires usually land before the measurement does." |
| **Leadership change** | LinkedIn job-change alerts, company announcements, team page diffs | Days 14 to 45. Before day 14 they are still in handover. | Their mandate, not their arrival. New leaders are buying, and they are buying in their first quarter. |
| **Hiring pattern** | Seek, LinkedIn Jobs, careers page. Read the roles as a set, not one by one. | 30 days from posting | What the roles reveal about the plan. Three growth roles at once is a strategy, and it is legible. |
| **Product launch** | Company blog, changelog, App Store or Play Store release notes, Product Hunt | Days 3 to 30 | The distribution question a launch creates. Building it and selling it are different problems. |
| **M&A or acquisition** | AFR, Crunchbase, company announcement | Days 14 to 60 | Integration. Two of everything, including two attribution setups that disagree. |
| **Tech stack change** | BuiltWith, Wappalyzer, job ads naming tools, a public migration post | 60 days | What the new tool does not solve on its own. Never assert their stack as fact, frame as observed from the public signal. |
| **Content publication** | Their post, podcast, talk, newsletter | Days 1 to 14. This one dates fastest. | Engage the argument. See bucket 1 in `personalisation-buckets.md`. This is the strongest single opener available. |
| **Award or press** | Industry awards, Fast 100, local business press | Days 7 to 45 | The pressure that follows attention. Everyone congratulates. Nobody asks what it changed. |
| **Market or office expansion** | LinkedIn posts, new location on the site, local press | Days 14 to 60 | The channel that does not travel. What worked in one market rarely ports. |
| **Website or positioning change** | Wayback Machine diff on the homepage, new messaging | 30 days | A repositioning is a decision someone fought for. It is a live topic internally. |

## Stacking

Two signals beat one by 4.7x on positive replies. A stack is only a stack when the second
signal explains or sharpens the first. Two unrelated facts in one paragraph read as a research
dump.

Combinations that carry a real argument:

- **Funding + hiring.** The round is the money. The roles are the plan. Together they say what
  the money is for, which is a question worth asking about.
- **Leadership change + tech stack change.** A new leader replacing a tool is mid-decision, and
  the decision has a budget attached.
- **Product launch + hiring for growth.** They built it and are now working out how to sell it.
- **Self-authored content + any company signal.** The strongest available. Their stated view,
  plus the company fact that makes the view urgent right now.

## Verification rule

Before a trigger goes in a draft:

1. **Date it.** Note the publication date. If it is outside the window, drop it and use a
   segment frame instead.
2. **Read the source, do not trust the aggregator.** Funding databases carry stale and wrong
   rounds. Job boards leave dead listings up.
3. **Check it is theirs.** Company-name collisions are common, and LinkedIn resolves company
   fields from the profile, which the HeyReach launch proved can be the wrong company for
   people with volunteer or advisory roles.
4. **Record the URL in the draft note.** Every trigger claim must trace to something checked
   this session, not to recall.

An unverifiable trigger is not a weak trigger. It is not a trigger.

## What is not a trigger

- A birthday, work anniversary, or follower milestone. Automated congratulation is the single
  most pattern-matched outbound shape on LinkedIn.
- A company being in your ICP. That is a filter, not an event.
- Their competitor doing something. Unless they have publicly responded to it, you are telling
  them what to worry about, which lands as arrogance from a stranger.
- A generic industry report. Everyone in the segment gets the same line and they can tell.
